The plain-language spine is taught laypeople-first, as a ladder of four examples that climb the abstraction: a thermostat, a bird catching a moth, a person crossing a street, and your own body. The unifying idea is that an organism keeps its predictions and its observations in line by changing the prediction *or* by changing the world. Inferring the hidden causes is the perception half; acting to make predictions come true is the active half; together they are "active inference." Extended outward, the lens offers a moral grammar the program uses publicly: distorted maps lead to extraction and harm, while accurate maps with calibrated signals and cooperation tend toward regeneration.

## The single most-misread point: minimizing VFE does NOT erase surprise (M14)

If a reader takes one correction from this chapter, it is this one, recorded as method claim **M14** (class: method, with an E-tier correction held for human review). Minimizing variational free energy does **not** reduce already-observed surprise. For a fixed observed outcome the surprisal `-ln p(o|m)` is what it is; minimizing free energy tightens an **upper bound** on it by improving the posterior approximation `q(s)` toward the true posterior `p(s|o,m)`. The anchor inequality is `F[q] >= -ln p(o|m)`, with equality exactly when `q(s) = p(s|o,m)`. Perception tightens the bound. Action lives one layer up, as expected free energy, and is deferred to a later layer of the framing; it is not what is happening when the bound is tightened.

The paired negative that travels with M14, and must be cited alongside it, is its provenance: this correction came from a **human science reviewer who would not sign off on the first draft**. The first draft got it wrong, in the ordinary and tempting way, by implying that doing inference makes the surprise itself go down. That is a measurement that the original framing was incomplete, and under this program a recorded negative of that kind is content, not an embarrassment to hide. The correction is held at the E tier (test-covered, negative-corrected) and would only be reopened if the inequality `F[q] >= -ln p(o|m)` were itself revised. Stating "minimizing VFE reduces already-observed surprise" anywhere in the encyclopedia is a falsification of this chapter's conceptual integrity, by definition.

## Why hierarchy needs the cavity principle (M22)

The last piece the lens contributes is **M22** (class: method), the **cavity principle**: a level in a hierarchy must never treat an upstream prior as if it were fresh evidence. The prior is divided out (the program records this as WS-B operating on the upward pass and WS-C on the downward pass) so that the same information is not counted twice. M22 also records that the factored mean-field approximation was **rejected as lossy**, so the exact joint posterior is used everywhere instead. The recorded violations that would falsify M22 are double-counting a prior as evidence, which inflates belief, or showing the mean-field variant to be non-lossy after all. The cavity principle is why the framing can stack layers at all without quietly manufacturing confidence out of nothing; it is a bookkeeping discipline, not a capability.
